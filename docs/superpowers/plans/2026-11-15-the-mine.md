# The mine on slice 1's frame (#660)

Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-slice-1-design.md`, "The mine", "What any screen has to meet" and "While a part is the end of the game"; prototype v12 (`366b890`) with its review fixes (`dfff521`, `a196897`), pictures in `docs/superpowers/specs/slice-1/`. Parent #530. Built inside the frame (#659).

## Rulings (agent-run sitting)

1. **Where it lives.** `mine-site` fills the frame's middle on the mine's site and hides on the city's. It paints a pure view, `mine-view`, and changes the game only through `progress.dispatch`. The scene is drawn by `mine-scene` on a canvas in art px. The − and + on a stage's row are the workforce sheet's own, moved out of it into `job-steps`, so the two never differ.
2. **The scene.** The prototype's look, which he approved, is drawn again in the game's code: the sky, the ground, the shaft, the headframe and the gallery in code, and the workers, the cage, the tubs, the drill, its dust and the sheave from the game's own sprites. No new art. The ground is painted once for a height, and the moving parts about ten times a second, on hard steps.
3. **The state is seen, not said.** `mineScene` works out, from the three stages' rates, where rock piles (the face, between drilling and haulage; the shaft, between haulage and hoisting), the share of tubs that leave the face full, the share of the time they can unload, each stage's busy share, and its machines that run or stand. Workers of a stage with spare capacity stand with a "z" for that share. The cage waits at the shaft bottom while hoisting has room to spare. Carts that do not run wait, empty, at the shaft end of the rail. Nothing says which stage limits the line.
4. **The level's plate** says "Topsoil", its rock a second (what the line moves) and the spec's line for one level, "4 crews · all rock is hoisted as stone". Under it, "Deeper strata: not yet surveyed. The city has to grow first.": the Granite is not named until part 3.
5. **The keys.** A key for each stage's strongest machine that is in play, shown and open: in part 1 the drill, the cart and the hoist. It says what it moves, its price and the workers it takes ("+Cart · 18/s", "600", "1 to run"). It follows the amount key, as the hire key does ("+10 Carts · 180/s", their total, "10 to run"), and buys with `tierBuyMove`. It is green when he can afford it. There is no hire key in the mine.
6. **The rows.** Each stage's row has its name, what the line moves now of what the stage can move, a band behind it that shows both against the strongest stage, who works with what, and its workers between − and +. A kind of machine shows the workers it has of the workers it takes ("1 of 2 hoists: 3 of 6 workers 1,200/s"), then the workers with tools ("2 on winches 200/s").
7. **Exact sums.** The parts are printed at the total's precision (a tenth while the total is under 100 and a part has a fraction, else whole) and rounded by largest remainder, so they always add up to the printed total. The row shows the fullest of three forms that fits: with "/s", without it, then terse ("2 carts 2/2 36").
8. **The third state** (rule 2.5, call 51): the stage has the workers for a machine and they stay on their tools because that moves more. The machine's part says it with both numbers, in the spec's proposed words: "2 carts stand · 2 workers move 6/s with barrows, 3.6/s on the carts". Its first number is a summand of the row. Too long for one line, it takes two. In the scene the machine stands with nobody asleep beside it. The words are Flemming's: asked as one question with pictures (`/mnt/project-files/slice-1/build/660-*.png`), and built as proposed meanwhile, behind the dev preview.
9. **A + with no free worker** shows "No free worker. Hire one, or take one off another job." over the keys. It goes when a worker is moved or hired, as in the workforce.
10. **A crew timer.** What a row moves now and can move are scaled by the crew timer (`crewTimer`, now exported from `rates`), so the rows agree with what is mined while one runs.
11. **Left for #661.** A tap on a row (its sentence and the stage's list) and a row's green lamp come with the stage's list. Until then a row is not a button.
