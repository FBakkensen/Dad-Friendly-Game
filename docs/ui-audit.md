# Screen audit

Measured on the running game by `npm run audit:ui` (`scripts/ui-audit.mjs`) at the phone, 424×924, portrait, device scale 2. That is the installed app on the OnePlus 15 and the control board's own size (#370): the board is drawn at scale 1 there. Other screens scale the whole board and are not audited; `npm run visual` checks the board at 424×924 and on the PC at 1280×800, 1366×768 and 1440×900. The audit records; it changes no style, layout, copy or play.

Shots live in [`docs/ui-audit/`](ui-audit/). Each file is `<screen>-<state>-<WxH>.png`. Prices, staff and other numbers in a shot are whatever the game showed when it was taken; today's economy numbers are in [Numbers now](DESIGN.md#numbers-now).

Bars used below:

- Touch targets 48×48px, with 8px between them (`--target-min`, `--target-gap` in `src/tokens.css`). [WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html) asks only 24×24 at AA. [Apple HIG accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) asks 44×44pt. 48px matches a Material touch target.
- Body text 4.5:1, large text and controls 3:1. [WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html). Large text here means 24px, or 18.66px at weight 700 or more.
- Spacing on a 4px grid. [Material 3 layout](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/understanding-layout/spacing) uses a 4dp step for the same reason: thumbs and type line up.
- No scroll at 424×924. The page (`html` and `#app`) never scrolls, and no sheet scrolls as a whole. Long lists scroll inside their sheet: late-game Buys and a full Upgrades inside their drawer (C1). The Workshop page fits with no scroll: three recipes a page (#374).

## What the report still flags

Every target is at least 48px, the page never scrolls, and no measured text pair fails contrast. What is left:

### Lists that scroll

The audit flags a drawer list that scrolls. Only late-game Buys and a full Upgrades scroll inside their drawer, as C1 allows (Crew, needs workers; Upgrades and boosts; Upgrades drawer). Mid-game Buys fits, Hazard in Buys included: a full Granite whose crew chips take two rows (#373). Buys' footnote stands over Back, and steps aside on an opening whose list it would push into a scroll.

### Targets under 48px or closer than 8px

None under 48px and none closer than 8px. The New sign over a key takes no taps (`pointer-events: none`) and stands in the band between the Ready now plate and the rail, so it covers no control. The audit measures a button only where a finger can reach it: the part inside its scrolling list, at rest, not mid-press. On the board (#370, #372):

- The relic plate's tag is a label, not a button: at 216×20 between the sockets and the stock row it could not be a 48px target. Each socket's note ends in a 48px `Relic shelf` key that opens the shelf.
- The mine window stands 8px below the boost dial. The bays stand 8px apart, the plate 10px from the red button, and the five keys of the rail (768px, #373) 16px apart; the rail ends 8px above the save line. An open drawer's rows stand 10px apart, and its "i" and Back are 48px plates.
- A bay's band tag is a label, not a button. The "Fixed! The crew cheers." note after a bay's fix lets taps through, so the bay under it stays in reach.
- The golden vein sits on the window's view, so its gap to the window is the one gap not asked for. It stands 8px or more from every other control at each end of its range.

`npm run visual` checks the board's own controls the same way, in board px, at 424×924 and the three PC sizes.

### Thumb reach

Every open sheet's main button is in the bottom half, except `Reload` on the update card (center 456px of 924) and the Ledger's Start over page, whose `Keep my mountain` and `Yes, start over` sit under its title at about 374px of 924 (#375). Keep has the focus. No Workshop page puts a `Craft` in the top third any more: the drawer's first recipe row sits under the plates and the bench, its `Craft` at about 472px of 924 (#374). On the board the mine window, the tap target, sits in the top third too (center 272px), where the B3 gauge row puts it (#371); it is 212×180, so a thumb reaches it from below.

### Spacing and colour

Off the 4px grid, most seen: 1px and 6px padding on `.teach-info` and the sheets' way-back buttons. The board's own spacing is in art pixels (2px), so 6px, 10px and 14px there are on its grid.

Near-duplicate night browns (RGB distance under 15): `#1e130d` / `#1d1611` / `#140d09` / `#120c0a` / `#0e0d0a`, `#3a2a1c` / `#362514`, and the ink pair `#c9b391` / `#c9b08e`. The board adds near twins of its own: amber `#e8a84a` / `#e8ac4c` and `#ffd38c` / `#ffe08a`.

The audit's census reads colours from CSS, so it cannot see the board's art behind the text. `npm run visual` reads the screenshot instead. It shoots each board legend's words (the odometer's `Stones`, the dial legend, the tag, the save words, and in the gauge row `Stones a sec`, the crew plate's `Crew`, figure and free count, `No. 2 Shaft` and the Legacy plate; under it the bays' band tag, each bay's name, sub-line, flap digits, price legend and price, the Ready now plate's three lines and the red button's legend, #372) with the text made clear, and asks 4.5:1 of the text colour against the 10th and the 90th percentile of the background's luminance. It reads the words' own extent, so `Stones a sec`, centred in a box as wide as the gauge, is read against the gauge face and not the bezel beside it. It reads the key rail's names and sub-lines, and in an open drawer every row's name, info, need, why, flag, gain, price and verb, the same way (#373). The enamel is textured, so a lone scratch does not fail a legend; the darkest and lightest pixels are printed beside it. `Stones` is drawn in the bare ink, as the dial's legend is: 5.87:1 at the percentiles, 4.16:1 at the lightest scratch.

### Copy

One `?` is left in player text: `Start over?` (`src/screen-words.ts`, on the Ledger's Start over page). Purchases, tending and crafting show real numbers. Relic hints, the layer hint, expedition time and homecoming finds stay vague.

## Information architecture

A session is either about 2 minutes (look, one decision, leave) or about 30 minutes (plan, craft, send people out, tune the crew). The short path stays short. The long path is one tap away, and it does not sit on the mountain while he is only checking in.

## Design direction

Flemming on the first token mocks: better, and not yet a crisp night. The notes below are the rules for three later mocks (`mountain-a` with `upgrades-a`, `mountain-b` with `upgrades-b`, `mountain-c` with `upgrades-c`). The game follows direction B, now as the control board: the key rail and the buy drawers (#373).

Looked at Cookie Clicker, Egg Inc, AdVenture Capitalist, Idle Miner Tycoon, Melvor Idle, and Universal Paperclips, plus the touch research already behind the 48px bar. Nine rules:

1. **One hero number, and the rate tucked under it.** Cookie Clicker's eye lands on the Big Cookie, then the bank and the cookies-per-second line beside it. The count and the rate are one thought. The next buy is the only other number at that volume. ([Big Cookie](https://cookieclicker.wiki.gg/wiki/Big_Cookie), [a walkthrough of that screen](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2591407770))
2. **The picture is the tap, and the tap answers.** The Big Cookie shrinks for a moment and a number floats off it. Our press is that same physical inset, and the price stays on the button. The layout does not jump, so the 8px gap survives the press. Sound stays off until he unmutes.
3. **The buy sits where the thumb already is. The count can sit up high.** He plays one-handed: the phone stays in one hand, and the thumb taps the mine window. Flemming picked the sheet for direction B. Read-only lines sit at the top: the pocket on the odometer, then the gauge row with the stones a second and the crew plate. The mine window sits in that row, in the top third of the screen (#371); at 212×180 it is a fat target a thumb reaches from below. The machine bays and the Ready row sit in the lower half, under the thumb: the Ready now plate buys the next machine in one tap, and the red DIG button digs beside it (#372). Buys, Workshop, Engineers, Upgrades and Ledger sit on the key rail, under the thumb. The buy drawers open over the board below the odometer, so a buy's spend shows on the drums. The New sign stands over its key and takes no taps, so it never covers the gauge row or the plate. A row's state is its lamp: ready (a green lamp, amber on the Ready now pick, the price and what one more adds), short of workers (`Needs N workers` in rust; the free count is on the crew plate; it still buys), too expensive (a dark lamp and the dim tag, the price only), or locked (`Locked` on the dim tag, no price). ([Hoober, UX Matters, 2013](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php), [Hoober, ACM Interactions, 2015](https://interactions.acm.org/archive/view/may-june-2015/fingers-thumbs-and-people), [thumb zones](https://gamedesign.gg/articles/mobile-game-ux-design/))
4. **An icon, a name, and one number.** Cookie Clicker's shop is a picture, a count, and a price, not a paragraph. A phone table that tries to show every column becomes a squint. The name stays, because a bare sprite is not a sentence. The sentence does not.
5. **Numbers use tabular figures.** Each digit takes the same width, so a ticking counter does not wobble. `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/font-variant-numeric))
6. **One gold control.** On the board it is the Ready now plate (#372): the next machine he can run and afford, lit amber, with its price and what it adds. Saving for dims it and names what he saves for. Everything he cannot buy yet uses the one disabled fill, or it leaves the check-in entirely. Two gold buttons is two decisions. A lit key cap is a status, not a second gold control: it says something waits behind that key, and the key opens a drawer; it never buys.
7. **Two sessions, two depths.** A two-minute open shows the hero number, the picture, and one buy, then he is gone. A thirty-minute sitting opens Workshop, Engineers, or Upgrades with one thumb tap, and that sheet fits. Portrait is the interruptible shape. ([sessions and portrait](https://gamedesign.gg/articles/mobile-game-ux-design/), and the target map above)
8. **Whitespace is the hierarchy.** Six equal cards make every fact shout. One frame, one gold button, and quiet type for the rest. The 8px gap and the 16px pause around the hero are doing that work. ([Material spacing](https://m3.material.io/foundations/layout/understanding-layout/spacing), [48px targets](https://m3.material.io/foundations/designing/structure))
9. **Free hands are counted once.** Spare workers are one pool, so the crew plate on the board is the only place that says how many are free (#371). A buy card says what that one machine needs (`Needs 3 workers`) and stops there.
