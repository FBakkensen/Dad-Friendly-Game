# Slice 1 prototype (throwaway)

A playable phone prototype of slice 1: the mine, the refinery, the factory and the city, from the first evening to the Foundry. It is the reference the slice 1 design points to for how the screens look and play (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-slice-1-design.md`, "What he sees"). It is not the game's code and nothing in `src/` uses it. The numbers are made up and run fast; the taps are real.

- `proto.src.html` is the whole prototype: styles, scenes drawn on canvas, and the fake economy.
- `node prototypes/slice-1/build.mjs` inlines the game's sprites and fonts and writes `page.html` (the bare page, for a host that adds its own document shell) and `index.html` (standalone, opens from a file or any static host). Both are git-ignored.

What Flemming played and settled in it (14 and 15 Nov 2026):

- Each site has its own scene and its own gesture. The mine is the mountain cut open (− and + on a level move a crew there). The refinery is rock falling through kilns and saws into the stockyard (tap a maker to add one). The factory is a station seen from above (drag a blueprint onto it). The city is a skyline that grows.
- No tap mines, and no tap ships goods. He orders a building once, one transport fleet (horse wagons, then trucks) carries the materials at its own rate, the builders build them in on the site, and a step is done when its materials are built in. A factory work order is done the same way, with no fixed timer.
- Unattended time runs at the night shift output until the stockyard is full, and transport keeps delivering to what he ordered. The shift report says what came in, and says "full" only when a stockpile was full.
- It starts on the first evening: one level, stone, the mine and the city. The Light Tower opens the refinery and the stockyard. The shaft opens the Granite, saws and the factory.
- He did not understand version 5's workforce plate ("What is that with hands", 15 Nov) and version 6's rewording was not enough. His principle since then: the game never tells the player the answer. It shows the numbers, and the player who works out the balance gets the advantage.
- Version 7 followed it with a table of the three stages: he hires each worker into a stage, and a machine takes workers of its stage to run it. He called it "still too explicit, but better" (15 Nov): the state should be visible in the scene, with something idle where a part has spare capacity or a buffer is full, and the same for transport.
- Version 8 shows the line in the scene. Rock piles up at the face and at the shaft where it arrives faster than it is taken, and the floor is bare where it is taken faster. Tubs run empty when the face gives too little rock, and queue when the shaft cannot take them. The cage waits at the shaft bottom when the hoist has spare capacity. Workers of a stage with spare capacity stand, with a "z" over them. In the city, wagons run as full as the stock lets them, and stand when there is nothing to carry. The table keeps only what a stage has ("3 drills ×600/s · 4 by hand ×100/s"), not what it adds up to, and no rule is spelled out. Next points only at gear that is done and at a building nobody has ordered, and otherwise counts what is affordable on each site. He said it was closer (15 Nov), but: he could not see what a stage actually moves a second, he does not understand "by hand" (said twice), the drillers looked asleep though drilling was the limit, and he could not see or move the workers. Workers should be one resource used for almost everything.
- Version 9 makes the workers one workforce. He hires into a pool and moves workers between jobs with − and +: drilling, haulage, hoisting, the kilns, the saws, driving, building. A count of workers and free workers sits beside the stock on every site and opens a list of all jobs. A machine, a kiln, a saw or a wagon stands until workers of its job run it ("1 of 2 drills"), and can be bought before there is anyone to run it. Transport brings materials to the building site, which holds 4,000, and builders build them in. Each stage shows what it moves now, and its workers stand in their own part of the level: hoisting at the shaft, haulage on the rail, drilling at the face. A worker without a machine works "with a pick", "with a barrow" or "on a winch". Upkeep for workers is a possible later step and is not built. The split between the levels is still four crews. He said "the workers do as expected" (15 Nov), so the workforce is settled, but the table of the three stages was "still not clear".
- Version 10 redraws that table. A stage's row says what it moves now of what it can move ("300 of 1,100/s"), with a band behind the row that shows the same. Under it, who works with what and what each group moves, as a sum that adds up to what the stage can move ("1 cart 800/s + 3 with barrows 300/s"). The number of workers sits between its − and +, under the word Workers, and a machine's key says how many it takes to run ("3 to run"). Tapping a row says the same in a sentence. He said it was "for sure better" (15 Nov), but "1 of 2 hoists" and "1 with a barrow" showed no reason: he guessed missing workers and could not see it.
- Version 11 puts the workers into that sum: the workers a kind of machine has, of the workers it takes ("1 of 2 hoists: 3 of 6 workers 1,200/s", "2 carts: 4 of 4 workers 1,600/s + 1 with a barrow 100/s"). The parts add up to the stage's workers, shown between − and + as "5 workers". To give the rows the width, the keys moved to one row above them: +Worker and one machine per stage. The refinery and transport say it the same way ("3 of 4 workers", "1 of 3 drivers"). He said "This is great" (15 Nov), with one change: hiring was only on the mine's page, which made workers look like a mine thing.
- Version 12 moves hiring to the top row of every site: the count of workers and free workers beside the stock (tap it for all jobs), and the +Worker key next to it. The mine's row of keys holds the three machines. With three stockpiles in that row, a pile shows what it holds and says "full" when it is; the refinery shows the capacities. This is the mine he approved, with hiring where he asked for it.
- Version 13 plays part 2 by the two rules he gave after version 12: every part runs on its own with a pile in front of it (15 Nov, 13:18Z), and the mine starts with one worker (13:15Z). The mine is a chain: drilling fills the drilled rock, haulage carries it to the shaft, hoisting lifts it. A pile holds 3,000, and its line sits in the row of the stage that fills it ("Drilled rock: 1,215 of 3,000"), as in the game since #703. A stage's row says what that stage moves now, so the three rows differ. It starts with one worker on drilling, no stone, a horse wagon with no driver and no builders. In the refinery the kilns have their own pile, "Rock at the kilns": the hoist fills it first, and rock that finds no room there goes to the stone stockpile ("bypass to stone 600/s"). The kilns' plate reads like a stage's row: "KILNS 200 of 200/s", "2 of 3 kilns: 2 of 3 workers", the pile, the workers between − and +, and "+Kiln 100/s · 17K · 1 to run". The hopper is as full as the pile, and a worker with no kiln, or whose kiln takes nothing, stands. A full brick stockpile stops the kilns, their pile fills, and all rock goes to stone. A full stone stockpile stops hoisting, then the shaft fills and stops haulage, then the drilled rock fills and stops drilling. A night ends that way, and the shift report says when each stockpile was full. If the line stood for another reason, the report gives the piles' numbers and no advice. Reset has a third start, "The roof goes on", a minute before the Light Tower's roof opens the refinery. The saws have a pile of the same kind. The levels are still shared by four crews.
- Version 14 plays the open question of part 3 (rule 5, before #673): drilling is one job on each level. The four crews of versions 4 to 13 go. Each level has its own row on the level itself ("TOPSOIL drilling 800 of 800/s", its workers between − and +), its own drilled rock and its own rock at the shaft, 3,000 each, with the two pile lines under the heaps. Haulage and hoisting stay one job each and serve both levels; when they cannot take everything, they share between the levels by what the piles offer. The drills are one fleet, and a new drill goes to the level with the most workers on picks. The table under the mine holds the three machine keys and the haulage and hoisting rows, so it no longer covers the second level. Sinking the shaft opens the Granite with nobody on it: he puts workers there himself. The workforce list has a Drilling group with one line per level.
