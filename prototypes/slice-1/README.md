# Slice 1 prototype (throwaway)

A playable phone prototype of slice 1: the mine, the refinery, the factory and the city, from the first evening to the Foundry. It is the reference the slice 1 design points to for how the screens look and play (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-slice-1-design.md`, "What he sees"). It is not the game's code and nothing in `src/` uses it. The numbers are made up and run fast; the taps are real.

- `proto.src.html` is the whole prototype: styles, scenes drawn on canvas, and the fake economy.
- `node prototypes/slice-1/build.mjs` inlines the game's sprites and fonts and writes `page.html` (the bare page, for a host that adds its own document shell) and `index.html` (standalone, opens from a file or any static host). Both are git-ignored.

What Flemming played and settled in it (14 and 15 Nov 2026):

- Each site has its own scene and its own gesture. The mine is the mountain cut open (− and + on a level move a crew there). The refinery is rock falling through kilns and saws into the stockyard (tap a maker to add one). The factory is a station seen from above (drag a blueprint onto it). The city is a skyline that grows.
- No tap mines, and no tap ships goods. He orders a building once, one transport fleet (horse wagons, then trucks) carries the materials at its own rate, and a step is done when its materials have arrived. A factory work order is done the same way, with no fixed timer.
- Unattended time runs at the night shift output until the stockyard is full, and transport keeps delivering to what he ordered. The shift report says what came in and what stopped it.
- It starts on the first evening: one level, stone, the mine and the city. The Light Tower opens the refinery and the stockyard. The shaft opens the Granite, saws and the factory.
- He did not understand version 5's workforce plate ("What is that with hands", 15 Nov) and version 6's rewording was not enough. His principle since then: the game never tells the player the answer. It shows the numbers, and the player who works out the balance gets the advantage.
- Version 7 followed it with a table of the three stages: he hires each worker into a stage, and a machine takes workers of its stage to run it. He called it "still too explicit, but better" (15 Nov): the state should be visible in the scene, with something idle where a part has spare capacity or a buffer is full, and the same for transport.
- Version 8 shows the line in the scene. Rock piles up at the face and at the shaft where it arrives faster than it is taken, and the floor is bare where it is taken faster. Tubs run empty when the face gives too little rock, and queue when the shaft cannot take them. The cage waits at the shaft bottom when the hoist has spare capacity. Workers of a stage with spare capacity stand, with a "z" over them. In the city, wagons run as full as the stock lets them, and stand when there is nothing to carry. The table keeps only what a stage has ("3 drills ×600/s · 4 by hand ×100/s"), not what it adds up to, and no rule is spelled out. Next points only at gear that is done and at a building nobody has ordered, and otherwise counts what is affordable on each site. He said it was closer (15 Nov), but: he could not see what a stage actually moves a second, he does not understand "by hand" (said twice), the drillers looked asleep though drilling was the limit, and he could not see or move the workers. Workers should be one resource used for almost everything.
- Version 9 makes the workers one workforce. He hires into a pool and moves workers between jobs with − and +: drilling, haulage, hoisting, the kilns, the saws, driving, building. A count of workers and free workers sits beside the stock on every site and opens a list of all jobs. A machine, a kiln, a saw or a wagon stands until workers of its job run it ("1 of 2 drills"), and can be bought before there is anyone to run it. Transport brings materials to the building site, which holds 4,000, and builders build them in. Each stage shows what it moves now, and its workers stand in their own part of the level: hoisting at the shaft, haulage on the rail, drilling at the face. A worker without a machine works "with a pick", "with a barrow" or "on a winch". Upkeep for workers is a possible later step and is not built. The split between the levels is still four crews. He said "the workers do as expected" (15 Nov), so the workforce is settled, but the table of the three stages was "still not clear".
- Version 10 redraws that table. A stage's row says what it moves now of what it can move ("300 of 1,100/s"), with a band behind the row that shows the same. Under it, who works with what and what each group moves, as a sum that adds up to what the stage can move ("1 cart 800/s + 3 with barrows 300/s"). The number of workers sits between its − and +, under the word Workers, and a machine's key says how many it takes to run ("3 to run"). Tapping a row says the same in a sentence. He has not played this one yet.
